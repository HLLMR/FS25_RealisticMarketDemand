## Title
Realistic Market Demand

## Short description (one-liner)
Buyers have a finite monthly appetite — flood a selling station with one crop and
its price steadily drops. Sell smarter to keep prices high.

## Description (long)

Realistic Market Demand makes selling feel like a real local market. Each selling
station tracks how much of each product you've dumped there this month, and pays
**progressively less** the more you sell. No cliffs and no "you can't sell here"
wall — just honest diminishing returns, like saturating a real buyer.

**What it does**
- Price drops continuously as you sell more of a fill type at a station within
  the in-game month.
- Every selling point tracks every crop independently — flooding wheat at one
  station doesn't touch silage at another.
- Demand resets each in-game month, so prices recover. Patience, or spreading
  sales across stations and crops, pays off.
- Your demand state is saved with your game.
- It layers on top of the base game's pricing rather than replacing it.

**Controls / hotkeys:** none — it works automatically once enabled.

**Settings:** none in this version; sensible defaults are built in. An in-game
tuning menu is planned for a future update.

**Compatibility**
- Single-player. (Multiplayer is not supported in this version.)
- PC only (contains scripts).
- Works across maps and selling stations; most likely to conflict with other
  mods that also rewrite selling-station pricing.

## Positioning note (not for the listing — for us)

A mod called **Global Market Forces** recently launched around dynamic economic
*cycles*, supply chains, and policy trends. We must not read as a duplicate. The
one-line differentiation to use in replies/README when the question comes up:

> Realistic Market Demand is the **simple, deterministic** option: it models one
> thing well — a single station's price sagging as you flood it with one crop
> that month, recovering next month. No economic cycles, no policy simulation, no
> configuration. If you want a light, predictable "don't dump everything in one
> place" mechanic that stays out of the way and plays nicely with other mods,
> this is it. If you want a full macro-economy simulation, that's a different
> (heavier) mod — and the two solve different problems.

Lead with **focused / deterministic / compatible / zero-config**. Never
disparage the other mod.
